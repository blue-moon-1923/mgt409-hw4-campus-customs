Check 2 — A category question rebuilds the page as a results grid

Asked in the chat panel: What t-shirts do you sell?

The site navigated itself to the Products page and replaced the full catalogue with a captioned results grid — heading “Yale T-shirts”, the original question shown underneath, and 25 products. Each card carries the photograph, garment type, name, price, a short description, colour swatches, and which sizes are in stock, and clicking any of them opens that product's own detail page.

Verified against the database. The catalogue contains exactly 25 products in the T-Shirts category and all of them are priced at $32, which is what the assistant said: “We have 25 T-shirts, and they're all $32.” This is the complete list, not a sample — the agent used the browse_category tool, which returns the whole category rather than the eight best matches a search would return.

Check 3 — Usability improvement from Problem 9: search, filter and sort

On the Products page: typed navy, chose the Hoodies category, and sorted by Price: low to high.

The toolbar narrowed 102 products to 25 of 102 and sorted them cheapest first ($45, $45, $68), with a Clear button appearing once a filter is active. Before this change the page was a single unsorted grid of 102 items with no way to search it.

Why the category filter is the interesting part. The three cards shown are filed in the database under three different labels — “hooded pullover sweatshirt”, “hooded sweatshirt”, and “pullover hoodie”. The raw garment_type column holds 22 labels for about six kinds of garment, so a dropdown built straight from it would have been unusable. One shared mapping folds them into six real categories, and the same mapping is used by the filter here and by the assistant's browse_category tool, so the page and the chat always agree.
